Product thinking cheat sheet

The short version of each lesson, for when you’re in a meeting. Based on the IDG Product Thinking pathway.

IDG’s three principles

  1. Focus on the problem, not the solution.
  2. Chase outcomes, not outputs.
  3. Integrate policy, ops and tech.

Is it a problem? Ask three questions

From Lesson 1: Solutions in disguise.

  1. Who are our users?
  2. What are they trying to do?
  3. What’s getting in their way?

Signs of a solution in disguise

  • It names a thing to build or buy: an app, AI, a bot, a system, a migration.
  • You can remove the people and the sentence still makes sense.
  • It mentions users but has already chosen the fix (“an app so citizens can…”).

The move

Before discussing the solution, ask the three questions. Then write the answers down without naming anything to build. Keep it narrow: one group, one problem.

Five Whys: from symptom to cause

From Lesson 2: Start with the whys.

The move

Start with the symptom. Ask “why?”, then ask “why?” about the answer, five times. Each answer should start with “because…”, never “we should…”.

Choosing the level to fix

  • Aim for a cause close to the root that you can act on within your timeline and resources.
  • Avoid fixing symptoms, and causes outside your sphere of influence.
  • Problems rarely have one root cause, so pick the best level for your team.

The 4Cs problem statement

From Lesson 3: The 4Cs problem statement. Four dimensions, one statement, no solutions yet.

Clarity

Who is affected, doing what, what’s broken, and how badly?

Consequence

What happens if it isn’t solved? “Not much” means it may not be worth solving.

Cause

Why does it exist? Use the result of your Five Whys.

Confirmation

What evidence shows it’s real, significant and worth prioritising?

Final check: does the statement describe the problem, or sneak in a solution?
